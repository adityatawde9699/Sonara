Make room for listening

Reports, research, books and notes do not always fit into a quiet hour with a screen. Sonara gives those documents a spoken form you can follow, pause and come back to.

It is a desktop reader built around the source document and its narration, not a new library to fill or a service that needs your files.

The document stays yours

Bring the formats you use

PDF, EPUB, DOCX, Markdown and plain text. Scanned PDFs are read with OCR, which ships with the app. Complex layouts can still extract imperfectly.

Choose a voice

Download Piper voices or Kokoro from Settings. Each file comes from a fixed upstream release and is checked against a pinned SHA-256 hash before Sonara uses it.

Follow the passage

The reading view highlights the passage being spoken. Play, pause, seek, change speed from 0.7× to 1.5×, and start from any paragraph. Sonara remembers where you stopped.

Review spoken changes

Preview the spoken-text layer before synthesis. Supported abbreviation and measurement expansions do not rewrite the source document.

Keep assistance optional

The document assistant is separate from reading and speech. Its provider is configured on its own and may be local or remote.

From file to listening

Four steps, each one close to the document and the controls you need.

  1. Choose a document

    Open a supported file with your system’s file picker.

  2. Choose a voice

    Pick a Piper or Kokoro voice you downloaded in Settings.

  3. Prepare the audio

    Sonara extracts the text and synthesizes it passage by passage on your computer.

  4. Listen and return

    Follow the highlighted passage, and pick up where you left off next time.

Nothing is rewritten in place

Sonara keeps the original text separate from supported pronunciation and measurement adjustments. Switch the example to see the difference.

Different tools, clear roles

Speech engines create the audio. Language models only power the separate assistant.

Piper

Default voice engine

Included in the app. Four American English voices, about 60 MB each, download from Settings.

Kokoro

Second voice engine

Included in the app. Its model and voice files, about 350 MB, download from Settings.

Assistant

Optional, not for speech

An embedded llama.cpp model, a local Ollama server, or OpenAI and Gemini. None of them is needed to read a document aloud.

Your document stays on your computer for speech

Text extraction, OCR, audio generation and playback all run on your computer. Voice models download only when you press Download in Settings, from fixed sources, and are checked before use.

Assistant providers are configured separately. If you choose an external provider and use an assistant feature, relevant document text may be sent to that provider. Review the provider and settings before use.

Text and audio stay in step

The player marks every passage of the document, and the current one is lit. Jump to any passage, or click a paragraph in the reading view to listen from there.

Questions, plainly answered

What is Sonara?

A desktop document reader that turns your files into narration on your own computer, with a reading view that highlights the passage being spoken and saves your place.

Which document types can I open?

PDF, EPUB, DOCX, Markdown and plain text. Scanned PDFs are read with the OCR included in the app. Extraction depends on the document’s structure.

Can Sonara work offline?

Yes, once you have downloaded a voice. Text extraction, OCR and speech run on your computer, and the runtime and tools they need ship inside the installer.

Which voices are included?

The installers include the Piper and Kokoro engines but no voice files. Four American English Piper voices (Ryan, Lessac, Bryce and John) and Kokoro download from Settings → Speech models.

Does Sonara send my documents to a service?

Not for reading or speech, which run on your computer. Assistant providers are configured separately; an external provider may receive document text when you use its assistant features.

Is there an installer for my computer?

Linux on x86_64 (.deb and AppImage) and macOS on Apple silicon (.dmg). The macOS build is not yet signed, so macOS asks you to approve it in System Settings the first time you open it. Windows builds are not available yet.
